Where did this contact come from? Options for context-aware contact cards using Delta Chat QR codes, a contextual inbox concept, and small printer notes. Research notes; nothing here is for sale.

The problem

You hand someone a card, they scan its QR code, and they can message you on Delta Chat. Weeks later a message arrives from a name you do not recognize: which event, which room, which card was this? A plain contact QR cannot tell you. This page compares four ways to keep that context, with their real tradeoffs, plus the printing side.

How a Delta Chat invite QR works

A Delta Chat invite is a link of this shape:

https://i.delta.chat/#FINGERPRINT&a=ADDRESS&n=NAME&i=INVITENUMBER&s=AUTH
https://i.delta.chat/#FINGERPRINT&a=ADDRESS&g=GROUPNAME&x=GROUPID&i=INVITENUMBER&s=AUTH   (group invite)

Everything after # is a URI fragment: it never reaches the i.delta.chat server, which only serves static files. The QR and the link carry the same data. If someone already has Delta Chat (or a compatible client), the app handles the code directly and no website is contacted at all. An invite works for many people, and resetting an invite invalidates the old one.

Sources: deltachat/interface uri-schemes.md, deltachat/invite, delta.chat/en/help.

Four ways to keep the context

A. One group per event exact context

Create a group named after the event, print its group-invite QR on the cards for that event. Everyone who scans lands in that named group, so the context is visible by construction.

  • Context is exact and automatic: arrivals appear in a group named after the event.
  • Zero per-card work; one QR serves the whole event.
  • Revocable: reset the group invite and old printed codes stop working.
  • Privacy cost: group members see each other, receive every message anyone sends, and all members have equal rights (anyone can add or remove members). Strangers from the same event become visible to each other.
  • Delta Chat's own help advises: groups are meant for trusted people, avoid sharing them publicly. A lost or photographed card can be scanned by anyone.

B. One private mini-group per person exact + private

Create a two-person group per card, print a unique QR per person.

  • Exact context and fully private; each arrival is its own chat.
  • Heavy setup: one group, one exported invite, one unique print per person. Label apps can batch-print unique QRs from a list, but creating each group is manual.

C. A separate profile just for cards private, coarse context

Delta Chat has first-class multi-profile support. Add a profile used only on printed cards and print its plain contact QR.

  • Every contact arrives as a private one-to-one chat.
  • Low friction: one QR design, no per-event or per-person setup.
  • Context is only "came via a card", not which event, unless you run several profiles.
  • Stock clients make you switch profiles manually; there is no unified inbox across profiles (see the kunjung concept below).

D. Plain contact QR + a printed line simplest

Print your normal contact QR plus a line asking the person to mention where you met.

  • Nothing to set up, nothing to revoke.
  • Context depends on the other person remembering to say it.

kunjung: a contextual inbox concept concept, not built

Multi-profile support in Delta Chat is real, but stock clients make you hop between profiles by hand. kunjung (from Indonesian mengunjungi, to visit) is the chosen name for a companion client idea: one inbox across your profiles, with each incoming conversation tagged by the profile and card context it arrived through. The Delta Chat core library already runs multiple accounts in one client process, so this is a client-UI project, not a protocol change. Status: name and idea only.

How big should the QR print?

A realistic invite link is about 165 characters, which encodes to a 49x49 module QR (version 8, medium error correction). At 203 dpi (8 dots/mm) that means roughly 4 dots per module at 25 mm wide, and a comfortable 5 at 30 mm. Guidance: keep the QR at 28-38 mm on the label; a 50x30 mm label works, 50x80 mm is comfortable. This is design arithmetic, not yet a physical print-and-scan test.

Open choices

  • Approach: A (group per event), B (private mini-group per person), C (separate card profile), or D (plain QR + a question line).
  • Media: monochrome sticker labels, or color photo cards.
  • Budget band: under Rp1 jt, Rp1-1,5 jt, or up to ~Rp4 jt.
  • Volume: a dozen cards per event, or 50+.
